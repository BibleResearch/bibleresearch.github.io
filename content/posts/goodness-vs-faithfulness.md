description: "We often conflate God's goodness with His faithfulness. While it would be a mistake to separate them too far, there is a Biblical distinction that ought to be maintained to avoid subtly imputing unfaithfulness to God."

When a Christian experiences a good circumstance and blessing, I often hear him or her say something like:

"God has been faithful."

We must use some caution here. Let's assume the blessing was something that God gave without promising to do so. Something like a healthy newborn child or remission of cancer. God never promised to give us either of these things and they aren't always given to faithful Christians.

Because God never promised to give us these things, the fact that God does give them isn't a revelation of His *faithfulness* - but His *goodness*.

An important, tangential question is how to think about God's goodness when He doesn't give these gifts, but this is beyond the scope of this post.

What complicates things in this distinction is that God promises to show His goodness to us in passages like Psalm 84:11 and 23:6. So when God gives good gifts, they are manifesting His goodness *and* His faithfulness to His promise to be good.

But it still stands that we should attribute an unpromised blessing from God first to His goodness - not His faithfulness.
