As demonstrated [here](https://bible.hightower.space/posts/2020-warning/), I've been walking through a trial of faith for some time now.

Here's an overview of what happened and where I'm at. There is a lot of ground to cover so I'm not going to get too into the weeds in this post.

The basic gist is that I have really been struggling with hermeneutics and epistemology. Can I be confident that my understanding of scripture is correct? I used to think the answer was a resounding "yes", but I'm not convinced that is the case anymore.

Back when I thought one could be absolutely confident in one's interpretation of scripture, I started to see what I perceive as a contradiction in the logic of that position which believes:

1. Scripture is highly perspicuous
2. We (as a denomination) clearly see belief A (this can represent any number of beliefs) in scripture
3. Belief A has never been consistently seen in scripture until very recently in Church history (e.g. mid-1800s)

I don't know how these views cohere without a contradiction. If scripture is perspicuous, one would not expect critical, foundational theology (e.g. dispensationalism) to have never been widely believed before the 1800s.

When I started asking this kind of question, I wasn't able to get any answers. Everyone can defend where they see such a belief in scripture, but no one seemed to be able to defend why we should even consider such a belief in the first place. In their minds, there was no possibility of being wrong about their understanding of scripture.

In the midst of questions and confusions about hermeneutics, I also started reading more about the different denominations that are out there.

Suddenly, I realized that there is a *massive* diversity of beliefs within Christendom on critical issues like the means of salvation, effects of salvation on a person, and the appropriate regular actions of a person who is saved.

This began to confirm my suspicions that it is not as easy to understand the Bible as I was originally told and had assumed. For example, I didn't (and don't) hold to baptismal regeneration. But the *vast* majority of Christians both today and throughout history have believed in baptismal regeneration. Why isn't there more clarity on such a critical issue? Is it 'safe' to be in the minority on this issue even if one sees it clearly from scripture?

This line of reasoning has permeated all of my theological thought.

I have much less confidence in my understanding of scripture than I did previously - and I really still don't know what to do with that.

Later I'll post more about where I'm landing, but wanted to share this for the time being.
